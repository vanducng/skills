# Insta360 X5 files and 360 reframing

From orbis-reframe's `docs/formats/insta360-x5.md` (read 2026-11-25), checked against real X5 clips with `ffprobe`.

## Files

| File | What is in it |
|---|---|
| `VID_<yyyymmdd>_<hhmmss>_00_<nnn>.insv` | 360 master: two fisheye video streams (one per lens, square, HEVC), AAC audio, then the `inst` trailer (calibration, IMU, exposure, GPS). |
| `LRV_<yyyymmdd>_<hhmmss>_01_<nnn>.lrv` | Proxy. For a 360 clip: one H.264 stream, both fisheyes side by side (1664x832 on the X5; lens 0, the `.insv` stream 0, is the right half). For a flat (single-lens) clip: a small flat picture. Same trailer. Fine for picking shots and for a soft 1080p reframe. |
| `VID_..._00_<nnn>.mp4` | Flat single-lens video. |
| `IMG_..._00_<nnn>.jpg` / `.insp` | Flat photo / 360 photo (both fisheyes in one JPEG). |

- The time in the name is the camera clock, local to wherever the camera was set. Pass `--clock-offset-hours` to `index` to get local time.
- The picture is full-range BT.709 (`yuvj420p`); ffmpeg handles it, keep `format=yuv420p` at the end of the chain.
- A recording is split every 29 min 59 s; the parts share the start time in the name.
- `index` reads a 2:1 frame as dual fisheye and shows it as a small equirectangular strip, so faces and the scene are readable on the sheet.

## Reframing with ffmpeg `v360`

`render` turns a dual-fisheye shot into a flat 16:9 view:

```
v360=input=dfisheye:output=flat:ih_fov=200:iv_fov=200:yaw=<deg>:pitch=<deg>:roll=<deg>:h_fov=<fov>:v_fov=<from 16:9>
```

| `view` key | Meaning | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| `yaw` | Turn left (negative) or right (positive). On the X5 proxy, 0 looks out of one lens, 180 out of the other (often the selfie side). | Try 0, 90, 180 and -90 on one frame and pick. |
| `pitch` | Up (positive) or down. | 0 to 10 |
| (camera holder) | On a selfie stick the holder's face sits at one yaw (on the X5 day 1 clip, yaw about 180). Point the view at what they look at; tag a face view `holder`. | |
| `roll` | Level a tilted horizon. | 0 |
| `fov` | Horizontal field of view; 75 to 90 looks natural, 100 already stretches faces and corridors at the edges (owner, 2026-11-26). `render` refuses over 95. | 85 |
| `yaw_end`, `pitch_end`, `fov_end` | Pan or zoom across the shot (eased). | a 30 to 60 degree pan over 4 s |
| `lens_fov` | Fisheye coverage per lens; 200 bare, about 195 with lens guards. | 200 |
| `type: "tiny-planet"` | Stereographic view looking down, pillar-boxed into 16:9. | a short shot to open or close |

Flat shots take `rotate` (90, -90, 180) for a camera mounted sideways, `roll` for a small tilt, `x`/`y` (0 to 1) to place the 16:9 crop and `zoom`.

`v360 input=dfisheye` assumes ideal equidistant lenses and no parallax. The seam between lenses can show, most on close subjects. Point the view away from the seam (yaw near 0 or 180 on the X5) for faces. For a calibrated stitch use orbis-reframe on Linux with a GPU, or Insta360 Studio, then cut the exported flat file with this skill.

## Flat (single-lens) X5 clips

The 1080p editing copies of flat X5 clips are barrel-distorted: straight lines bow near the edges. Set `"lens": "x5"` in the edit list: `lenscorrection` k1 0.12, k2 0.015, then a 14% crop, the smallest that leaves no empty edge (a white frame through the same chain stays white to the border). Tested on day 1 Taipei footage, 2026-11-26.
